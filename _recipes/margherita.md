## Sauce

1. **Crush by hand.** Tip the [[tomatoes]] tomatoes into a bowl and squeeze them through your fingers. Leave it chunky; don't blend.
2. **Season.** Mix in some salt, a splash of olive oil and a few torn basil leaves. Taste and add more salt if it needs it.
   - If the can is very watery, pour off some of the thin juice first, or the center of the pizza will go soggy.

## Prep the cheese

Fresh mozzarella holds a lot of water. Tear it into chunks about **an hour ahead** and let it drain in a strainer (or on paper towels) in the fridge. Pat dry before using.

## Build

1. **Stretch** the dough ball to about 12 inches, keeping a puffy rim.
2. **Top in this order:** spread the sauce thin, leaving the rim bare. Add a few basil leaves, sprinkle pecorino, drizzle olive oil, then scatter the mozzarella. Keep it light in the middle.

## Bake: outdoor oven

1. **Preheat to 800–900°F on the stone**, checked with an infrared thermometer.
2. **Bake 60–90 seconds**, turning the pizza every 15–20 seconds so the side by the flame doesn't burn.
3. **Done** when the rim is puffed with leopard spots and the cheese has melted.

## Bake: home oven

A home oven can't get close to outdoor-oven heat, so the crust will be paler and a bit crisper. It's still a good pizza.

1. **Preheat at the oven's max (500–550°F) for 45–60 minutes** with a baking steel or stone on the top rack, about 6 inches below the broiler.
2. **Bake 6–8 minutes**, until the bottom is browned.
3. **Switch to broil for the last 1–2 minutes** to color the rim and top. Watch it closely; it goes fast.
   - With the longer bake, fresh mozzarella can release water. Add it halfway through the bake if the center keeps going soggy.

## Finish

Top with a few fresh basil leaves and a little more grated pecorino. A drizzle of balsamic glaze is really good on this too.
